# Synthesis — after Steps 1-3 (you do this, not the model)

After the Panel Reviewer, Devil's Advocate, and Writing Coach have
all produced their outputs, you (the applicant) merge them. The
model can summarise on request, but the synthesis discipline below
is yours to apply.

The point is to leave the workshop with the residual issues — the
ones humans uniquely judge — not a thirty-finding document.

## Read in this order

1. **Panel Reviewer first.** It carries the structural diagnosis:
   which sub-questions are at risk, which caps fired, what would
   score the proposal B or C at Step 1. This is the load-bearing
   critique.
2. **Devil's Advocate second.** Read each counterargument against
   the Panel Reviewer findings. If a counterargument overlaps a
   Panel Reviewer finding, the agreement is high signal — fix
   first. If a counterargument is novel, use judgement: does it
   reveal a panel objection the Panel Reviewer missed, or is it
   reaching?
3. **Writing Coach third.** Read each prose edit. Most will be
   quick wins. Apply the ones that improve a tired reader's
   chance of finding the load-bearing claim early.

## Decision rules

- **Two roles converge on the same issue → inspect it first.**
  Treat convergence as a priority signal, not a verdict. All three
  roles run in one model and one chat session, and the Devil's
  Advocate has already read the Panel Reviewer's findings, so
  agreement is corroboration within a single reader, not independent
  replication. It tells you where to look first; it
  does not tell you the criticism is right. Your own scientific
  judgement decides that.
- **Only the Panel Reviewer flagged it → almost always fix it.**
  Caps that fired are not negotiable; structural omissions kill
  panel scores.
- **Only the Devil's Advocate flagged it → use judgement.**
  Devil's Advocate is reaching by design. Counterarguments
  worth raising are ones where a panel member would actually
  raise them, not every plausible alternative.
- **Only the Writing Coach flagged it → quick fix if cheap,
  defer otherwise.** Prose edits add up but are not panel-killers.
  Do the easy ones; do not let polish delay submission.

## What to bring to the workshop

Use the same workshop hand-off as the Basic pre-review, with two
adjustments:

- Up to three unresolved questions for human judgement, as Basic
  specifies.
- Add a one-line note that the Standard sequence was used: "AI
  pre-review (Standard) flagged N Panel Reviewer findings,
  K Devil's Advocate counterarguments, M Writing Coach edits.
  Items addressed: \[...\]. Items I want human judgement on
  (≤ 3): \[...\]."

Do not forward the raw three outputs to the workshop reader. The
reader does not need to see thirty findings; they need to see
your synthesis of which residual items want their judgement.

## What the model should not do during synthesis

If you ask the model to "synthesise the three outputs", it may
default to a neutral summary that loses severity weighting.
Instead, ask it to produce the workshop hand-off paragraph
constrained to your three highest-confidence unresolved items
— and tell it explicitly which items are unresolved. The model
is good at compression, not at choosing which items matter to
you.

## Time budget

- Step 1 (Panel Reviewer): about five minutes.
- Step 2 (Devil's Advocate): about five minutes.
- Step 3 (Writing Coach): about five minutes.
- Step 4 (your synthesis + revision decisions): about ten
  minutes, longer if you actually apply edits while reading.

Standard is heavier than Basic. If you have run out of time, run
only Basic.
